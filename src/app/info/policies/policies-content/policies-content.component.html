UCU Libraries and Archives

Repository policy framework

These policies integrate the Uganda Christian University Digital Institutional Repository into responsible research practice, academic recognition, open scholarship, and long-term stewardship.

Purpose, scope and compliance

This framework applies to research outputs, theses and dissertations, research data, software, learning resources, creative works, and approved institutional records deposited in the repository. It is read with UCU statutes, research-integrity and ethics processes, the repository terms of use, and the conditions displayed on each repository item.

UCU Libraries and Archives applies these policies in a manner consistent with applicable Ugandan law, including copyright and data-protection law, national research-ethics guidance, funder and publisher conditions, and relevant international commitments and standards. Where obligations conflict, the stricter lawful requirement and a documented case-by-case assessment apply.

Open access and deposit policy

UCU supports open access to research as widely as lawfully, ethically, and practically possible. Researchers, postgraduate students, and responsible UCU units must deposit eligible research outputs and the associated metadata through approved workflows as soon as practical after acceptance, award, completion, or publication.

For output types identified by UCU research and academic governance, repository deposit is the authoritative institutional record for research reporting, assessment, promotion, performance review, accreditation evidence, and public dissemination. Deposit requirements do not replace academic peer review, ethics approval, or publisher obligations.

The preferred deposit version is the publisher-permitted version, such as the accepted manuscript where the published version cannot be made openly available. Embargoes are recorded and managed so that metadata remains discoverable and files are released when lawful and appropriate.

Open metadata and interoperability policy

Accurate repository metadata is a public scholarly infrastructure. Unless a record contains personal, confidential, security-sensitive, third-party, or otherwise restricted information, UCU releases the rights it holds in original descriptive metadata under the CC0 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication. This dedication applies to metadata only; it does not licence item files, images, data, software, or other content described by the metadata.

Records should expose persistent identifiers and machine-readable metadata through appropriate open standards and protocols, including Dublin Core, OAI-PMH, persistent URLs, controlled vocabularies, and identifiers such as DOI, Handle, ORCID, ROR, or funder identifiers where assigned. Metadata should make clear the resource type, version, contributors, dates, licence, rights statement, access status, relationship to other outputs, and recommended citation.

The repository follows the FAIR principle of being as open as possible and as closed as necessary. It seeks interoperability with scholarly discovery services and repository networks while preserving UCU's authority to correct metadata, protect sensitive information, and apply appropriate access controls.

Rights retention, copyright and licensing policy

Authors retain copyright unless they have assigned it elsewhere. By depositing material, a depositor grants UCU a non-exclusive, worldwide permission to store, preserve, reproduce for preservation and access, and make the material available under the selected access conditions. The depositor confirms that they have authority to grant that permission.

Researchers should retain sufficient rights to meet UCU, funder, and open-access requirements before signing publishing agreements. UCU Libraries and Archives provides practical guidance on licences, accepted manuscripts, embargoes, permissions, and rights statements, but cannot provide legal advice or override a publisher agreement.

Research data, software and sensitive-content policy

Data, software, protocols, code, and documentation that underpin UCU research should be deposited or linked where this improves verification, reuse, education, and public benefit. Deposits must include sufficient contextual metadata, documentation, provenance, version information, and an appropriate licence or access statement for meaningful reuse.

Personal data, human-participant data, confidential information, protected community or cultural knowledge, commercially sensitive material, and security-sensitive content must not be made openly available without a lawful basis, appropriate consent, and any required ethics, contractual, or community approvals. Metadata and access conditions may be published while files are restricted, embargoed, anonymised, or deposited elsewhere under controlled access.

Integrity, versioning and withdrawal policy

Each published record should identify the resource and its version clearly. Corrections, updated files, related outputs, retractions, and withdrawals are recorded transparently where possible. Persistent links are retained unless legal, ethical, security, or privacy risks require otherwise.

UCU may restrict or withdraw files where there is a substantiated concern about copyright, confidentiality, research misconduct, fabrication, legal obligation, safety, or serious metadata error. The repository will use the notice and takedown process and maintain a public bibliographic record where doing so remains lawful and safe.

Preservation, governance and review policy

UCU Libraries and Archives is responsible for repository stewardship within approved resources and infrastructure. Preservation activities include curation, metadata management, integrity monitoring, format-risk review, backups, controlled access, and preservation planning as set out in the preservation policy.

Repository policy is governed collaboratively by UCU Libraries and Archives with relevant academic, research, information-technology, legal, records, ethics, and data-protection stakeholders. Policies are reviewed at least every three years, or earlier when legislation, UCU governance, funder conditions, technology, or material risks change.
